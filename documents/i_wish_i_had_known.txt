TITLE:
I Wish I Had Known

SOURCE:
CU Boulder Orientation
https://www.colorado.edu/orientation/i-wish-i-had-known

CONTENT:
Many students and families experience similar challenges during the transition to college, including increased independence, academic adjustment, and building new social connections. CU Boulder collected advice from families about what they wish they had known before their student started college.

Advice Before Attending CU Boulder

Students often experience a combination of excitement, stress, uncertainty, and anticipation before beginning college. Families are encouraged to discuss both the positive and difficult aspects of the transition.

Many students experience homesickness, academic pressure, and emotional challenges during the first semester. Open communication and emotional support can help students navigate these changes.

Parents are encouraged to express confidence in their students, remind them that support is available, and recognize that college is a significant step toward independence and adulthood.

Key themes include:
- Expect emotional ups and downs during the transition.
- Keep communication open between students and families.
- Encourage independence while remaining supportive.
- Understand that adjustment takes time.

Experiences During the First Year

Many families reported that the social and emotional aspects of college were more challenging than expected. Students often face:
- Making new friends.
- Living with roommates.
- Feeling lonely at times.
- Adjusting to new environments.
- Managing increased academic expectations.

Students may struggle during their first year academically, socially, or emotionally. However, many families noted that these challenges often improve as students gain experience and confidence.

Academic Success

Students are encouraged to use campus resources when they need help.

Helpful resources include:
- Tutoring services.
- Study groups.
- Academic support programs.
- Faculty office hours and meetings with professors.

Several families emphasized that students who seek academic support early are often better able to overcome challenges and improve their performance.

Supporting Student Growth

Families noted that one of the most important parts of the college experience is allowing students to develop independence and problem-solving skills.

Common advice includes:
- Avoid solving every problem for students.
- Encourage students to think through solutions themselves.
- Offer support and guidance rather than immediate answers.
- Allow students to learn from mistakes and challenges.

Staying Connected

Students often appreciate encouragement from home, especially during difficult periods.

Families reported that:
- Regular communication can help students feel supported.
- Care packages, messages, and video calls can reduce feelings of loneliness.
- Emotional support remains important even as students become more independent.

Key Takeaways

The transition to college involves academic, social, and emotional adjustments. Students may experience homesickness, stress, loneliness, and academic challenges during their first year. Families can help by providing encouragement, maintaining communication, supporting independence, and encouraging students to use campus resources when needed. Many families reported that the first year can be difficult, but students often become more confident, successful, and independent over time.
